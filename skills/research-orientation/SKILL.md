# Orient before extending

Read the objective and current progress, then the actual proof, code, data, or source passage relevant to the next claim. A README, old chat, or model summary is a map, not the underlying evidence.

Recover the exact object, quantifiers, allowed assumptions, and parameter domain. Identify which statements are definitions, checked examples, proposed lemmas, proved subcases, or still open. If two notes disagree, preserve both versions until the evidence settles the conflict.

Construct a small research map: established facts with file references; the decisive unknown; promising routes and their prerequisites; failed routes with the scope of the failure. Make this useful for choosing one next action, not an encyclopedia of all related mathematics.

Before searching broadly, inspect local references and existing calculations. Seek literature when the next step depends on a named theorem, an unfamiliar structure, a possibly known classification, or a technique your current derivation cannot justify. Read the theorem and hypotheses rather than borrowing a search snippet. If search access is unavailable, record the missing source without inventing it.

Save the map in research/PROGRESS.md. Imported notes begin as historical claims until checked; they do not inherit their author's confident language.
